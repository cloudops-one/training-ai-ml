# Module 11 · Capstone & Next Steps

**ShopWave asks:** *"Show us what you can do."*

**Time:** about 20 hours. **Prerequisites:** Modules 00–10. You do not need every optional lesson.

## Where you are

You have spent a year's worth of ShopWave problems learning one tool at a time: reports, spend prediction, churn, segments, forecasts, image tagging, a support chatbot, a deployed model. Every one of those came with a brief, a scaffold and a solution to compare against.

A capstone has none of that. **You choose the problem, you find the data, you decide what "good" means, and you defend your choices.** That is what the job actually is, and it is what an interviewer wants to see: not that you can follow a notebook, but that you can start from a vague question and finish with something someone could use.

## How the capstone differs from the mini-projects

| | Mini-projects (Modules 01–10) | Capstone |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Given | You choose and scope it |
| Data | Provided, already cleaned once | You find it, check its licence, write the download script |
| Scaffold | TODO cells in order | An empty structure; you decide the steps |
| "Good" | A rubric for that lesson | A baseline you set, a metric you justify |
| Audience | You | Someone who has never seen this course |
| Result | A notebook | A repository, a model card, a demo or deployment plan, a 5-minute talk |

## What you will be able to do afterwards

- Turn a vague business question into a scoped ML problem with a user, a decision, a target, a baseline and a metric.
- Run a project end to end without a scaffold, and know when to stop.
- Write a README, a model card and a short presentation that make your work understandable in five minutes.
- Answer the common ML interview questions clearly, with examples from your own work.
- Choose what to learn next.
